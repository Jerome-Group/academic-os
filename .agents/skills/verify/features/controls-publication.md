Admission checks cover independent 8 MiB raw-input, individual-file and encoded-event limits.
LF counts toward the event limit. A raw input at its ceiling can still exceed the exact create,
plan or intent limit. Refused create staging stays unchanged. A refused intent retains its
unselected plan, leaves the selector unchanged, permits a distinct publication, and cannot
bypass a later stale-head refusal. Wire checks distinguish equal base64 character lengths
whose decoded lengths fall on opposite sides of the raw ceiling.

Native fixtures append and read beyond 64 MiB in bounded windows. Forged, expired, cross-file,
reordered, changed-size, changed-time and changed-link snapshots refuse. Replay checks strict
UTF-8 and JSON, canonical bytes, schema versions, hash chains, LF boundaries and torn tails.
Synthetic terminal-counter state verifies exhaustion without constructing an impossible-sized
history. The event-envelope test binds later record sizes to the native producer and schema
bounds; maintain it when their encodings or record shapes change.

These fixtures use separately compiled, fixed private anchors. They do not open production
publication authority or admit an existing Module. Native syscall, account, filesystem and
cooperative-writer assumptions remain runtime prerequisites. History retains O(history) parsed
records and scanning work; streaming grants neither bounded lifetime memory nor reserved disk
space. Unbounded repeated deaths can exhaust representable counters or disk and retain recovery
uncertainty. This profile supplies no publisher formal theorem or independent product verdict.
